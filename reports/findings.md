# Findings and interpretation

## Where asking rates differ

Among localities with at least 20 qualifying apartment listings, Sector 42 has the highest median asking rate at approximately ₹56,698/sqft (59 listings). Sector 105 and Sector 4 Sohna both sit at ₹3,600/sqft, based on 25 and 20 listings respectively. This is a comparison of advertised rates, not an estimate of price appreciation or investment return.

The ranking by rate/sqft is different from the ranking by total asking price. For example, Sector 24 has a median apartment asking price of ₹10 crore in the premium-locality table despite ranking below several other sectors by rate/sqft. Apartment sizes and listing mix deserve closer inspection before interpreting that difference.

## Size and BHK

Apartment area has a strong positive Spearman association with total asking price (0.797), but a weaker association with asking rate/sqft (0.378). The relationship is descriptive and does not isolate location, amenities or construction quality.

Among BHK groups with at least 20 qualifying records, 3 BHK has the most listings (5,214), followed by 4 BHK (2,899). Median total asking price rises across the observed BHK groups, from roughly ₹0.23 crore for 1 BHK to ₹12.87 crore for 6 BHK. The 6 BHK group contains only 39 listings.

## RERA and construction labels

Listings marked RERA-approved have a median asking rate of approximately ₹15,536/sqft versus ₹12,420/sqft for listings marked not approved. This difference does not show that approval itself causes a price premium.

Under-construction apartments have a median asking price of ₹2.945 crore, compared with ₹2.70 crore for ready-to-move apartments in the analyzed subset. Project and locality composition may explain some of the difference.

## Data-quality notes

- The dataset contains unusual area and BHK values, including plot identifiers that resemble bedroom counts; these were investigated during cleaning.
- 120 listings were flagged for an advertised-versus-calculated price/sqft discrepancy greater than 10%.
- Arithmetic consistency is not proof that an asking price is genuine. High-rate outliers need source verification.
- This is a cross-sectional listing analysis; it cannot establish price trends, transaction volumes or rental yields.
